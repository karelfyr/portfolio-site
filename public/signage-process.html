    Ideas from both. Taste from Karel.

    Decided by Karel

    • Images exactly as uploaded

      Never compressed, always in order, the first one is the hero – written into the project rules on day one.

    • Fidelity over performance

      Shortcuts keeps its real Safari glass; nothing simplified per browser.

    • An own booking system

      Instead of cal.com: a calendar, emails and a booking flow built for the site.

    • The feel of every motion

      The Decathlon and Shortcuts clips, tuned frame by frame – and a motion playbook to keep the taste reusable.

    • Every line of copy

      From “Engineer with a designer’s eye” to the signage offer and its prices.

    • 52 times: back

      Every version that didn’t feel right was reverted – the taste filter on everything AI proposed.

    Proposed by AI, kept by Karel

    • Bake the Decathlon clip to video

      After the live animation stuttered in Safari: polish it in code, then record it.

    • The GPU rendering path

      Smoother camera moves and zooms in the animated clips.

    • The booking logic

      Free slots from the calendar, confirmation emails, the server behind the popup.

    • One spacing scale, one grey scale

      And the mobile audit that followed: tabs, zero-shift switches, 44px targets.

    • Pricing with real numbers

      Raising the top plan to $11,900; US salary data for the comparison.

    • Browser plumbing

      Page locked under popups, the Safari pinning fix, cards and buttons on one edge.
